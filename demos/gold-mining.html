This is a condensed, playable replica of the laboratory experiment in Anticipatory Utility as a Buffer Against Consumption Delays (solo-authored; N = 360, University of Melbourne experimental economics lab). You will go through the screens participants saw, in order: the attention pledge, the mining-game instructions, the incentive-compatibility training, the soil-sample information offer, the waiting task, and — depending on a random draw — an unexpected delay before you learn what the nugget is worth.

  • Condensed: 2 brain-teaser rounds instead of 15, and waits of seconds instead of minutes. Everything else follows the original page sequence.
  • Faithful: instructions, artwork, elicitations, comprehension checks, and payoff rules are taken from the original oTree code.
  • Private: this page runs entirely in your browser — nothing you do here is recorded.

Like the real participants, you will be randomly assigned to the No-Delay or Delay condition. Toggle Researcher notes (top right) to see what each screen measures, or Skip to debrief at any time.

Overview of Study(Please read this page carefully)

In this experiment, you will be playing a gold mining game in which a miner will be working on mining some gold for you. Please read all the instructions carefully. A clear understanding of the instructions is critical for making decisions and earning money in the experiment.

There are four parts to the experiment. You will receive instructions for each part and will also be asked to complete a short survey after Part 4.

In this experiment, all payments are expressed in Experimental Currency Units (ECU). Your payments will be converted to Australian dollars (AUD) at a rate of 50 ECU = 1 AUD and will be paid to you privately at the end of the experiment.

You will receive 250 ECU for participating in the experiment, and you may earn additional payments throughout the experiment.

Researcher note A real-stakes lab study: incentive-compatible payments at 50 ECU = AU$1 plus the 250 ECU participation fee, credited in the earnings ticker above — exactly as participants saw it.

In this experiment, you will participate in a gold mining game. The rules are simple:

  1. A miner will work on mining some gold for you.
  2. The mining will take some time, and in the end, the miner might find nothing or a valuable gold nugget.
  3. All you need to do is wait for the mining to be completed.

Part 1 - The mining game(Instruction 3/3)

For the mining process, a miner randomly picks a spot to start digging. Whether the miner finds a gold nugget depends entirely on the location chosen.

In other words, once the miner picks a spot, the outcome of the mining is determined, and there is nothing you can do to change it.

The mining process should take about 10 minutes, but it may take longer.

Researcher note · the framing The gold-mining frame gives every participant an identical near-future "utility event" of uncertain size — a nugget worth 680–865 ECU — with the outcome fixed the moment the miner picks a spot. That last sentence matters: it makes all later information non-instrumental by construction. Nothing you learn can change what happens.

Now, you will decide how much you are willing to pay for the item, if:

  • The amount you are willing to pay ≥ The price the person wrote down ⇒ You will get the item and pay the price the person wrote down
  • The amount you are willing to pay < The price the person wrote down ⇒ You will not get the item and will not pay anything

Researcher note · primary measure This is the study's primary anticipation measure: willingness to pay to see the soil sample — pure, non-instrumental early information about the (already determined) outcome. Because it runs through the BDM mechanism against the miner's random price (0–50 ECU), truthful bidding is optimal, and a positive bid reveals utility from anticipating the news, not from acting on it.

Congratulations, the miner has found a gold nugget!

However, the value of this nugget remains uncertain and can be anywhere between 680 and 865 ECU.

Before the value of the gold is determined, you now have the opportunity to sell it to a buyer for cash.

Now, how much are you willing to accept to sell the gold nugget?

Please enter a whole number between 680 and 865.

Note that the gold will only be sold if the buyer's offer is higher than or equal to your price. In that case, he will get the gold nugget and pay you the price you've selected above; otherwise you get to keep the gold.

Researcher note · outcome measure The dependent variable: willingness to accept for the (still-uncertain) nugget, elicited with a BDM-style buyer offer — the very mechanism you were trained on in Part 1. Comparing WTA between the No-Delay and Delay conditions — and how that gap varies with the anticipation measures — is the paper's core test.

What you just did — and what it measures

How the real experiment works

  • Design: N = 360, between-subject. The waiting task ran 15 rounds (40 s each); Delay participants then faced an unexpected extra 8-minute wait before the reveal. Delay was randomly assigned, so its effect on valuation is causal.
  • Anticipation measures (triangulated): willingness to pay to see the soil sample (primary, BDM-elicited, non-instrumental by design), frequency of checking the mining status, and self-reported excitement in the post-task survey.
  • Headline result: delays reduce the valuation of the gold nugget on average — but the effect is concentrated among low-anticipation participants. High-anticipation participants barely discount at all: anticipatory utility buffers against consumption delays.
